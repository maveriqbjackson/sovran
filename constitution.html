The Constitution

How we keep the Charter true.

The Charter is what SOVRAN cannot do to you. This is the rulebook for everyone who works here, and every company we hire, so the Charter never quietly stops being true. It's public on purpose: you should be able to see the rules we hold ourselves to.

What this is

The Charter (mysovran.online/charter.html) is our promise to the public: what SOVRAN cannot do to the people who use it.

This Constitution is the promise we make to each other: how everyone inside SOVRAN works so the Charter stays true. It binds the founder, every employee, every contractor, every volunteer, and every company we hire. No job description, contract, budget decision or growth goal outranks it. If anything conflicts with this document, this document wins.

One test sits under every rule below: would this let anyone, inside or outside SOVRAN, learn who a person is, what they are worried about, or what they did on our site? If the answer is yes or maybe, the answer is no.

Article I: The Charter binds everyone inside

  1. Every article of the Charter is an internal rule, not just a public statement. Breaking one is breaking this Constitution.
  2. No one may build, buy, switch on or ask for anything that would make a Charter promise untrue, even for a test, even for a day.
  3. When a decision is unclear, the more private option wins. Whoever wants the less private option has to prove it is needed. Nobody has to prove the private option is good enough.
  4. Convenience, growth, revenue and "everyone else does it" are never reasons to collect more.

Article II: Data rules for every person in SOVRAN

These apply to the website, the apps, email, support, marketing, research and anything else carrying the SOVRAN name.

1. We do not learn who people are. No one may collect, request, infer or store the identity of anyone who visits our sites, uses our tools, or contacts us. That includes names, home addresses, phone numbers, device fingerprints, ad IDs, precise location, and social media profiles.

2. We do not watch what people do. No tracking of individual visitors in any form:

  • no analytics that follow a person from page to page or visit to visit,
  • no tracking pixels, web beacons, or open or click tracking in email,
  • no third-party cookies, and no first-party cookies used to recognize a returning visitor,
  • no session recording, heatmaps or "user replay" tools,
  • no embedded widgets that load a third party before a person chooses to click (share buttons, social feeds, video players, chat bubbles, maps).

3. We do not sort people into groups. No profiles, segments, personas built from real visitor data, interest categories, "lookalike" audiences, or any label applied to a person or a device. Describing our audience in general terms for planning is fine. Labeling actual visitors is not.

4. We do not share or sell lists. No list of people, emails or devices is ever uploaded to an advertising platform, data broker, "customer match" tool or partner, hashed or not.

5. Counts, never people. Where SOVRAN needs a number to stay running (abuse limits, uptime, how many people used a tool today), it records a count with nothing attached that could point to a person, and keeps it no longer than needed.

6. Messages to us. When someone writes to SOVRAN, we use what they sent only to answer them. We do not add them to anything, look them up, enrich their details, or keep the message longer than the conversation needs. Someone contacting us from outside the United States is treated exactly the same: nothing is gathered about them because of where they are.

7. Staff accounts. Personal accounts, browser extensions and AI tools may never be used to handle SOVRAN user information. Work happens only in tools approved under Article III.

Article III: Vendor and partner standard

Every outside company, freelancer, agency or tool SOVRAN pays or uses must meet this standard before it touches anything with the SOVRAN name, and keep meeting it.

1. Must:

  • Collect nothing about our visitors or users beyond what the job strictly needs, and nothing that identifies a person.
  • Agree in writing that it will not track, profile, segment, enrich, sell, share or retain data about anyone it reaches on our behalf.
  • List every sub-vendor that will touch SOVRAN work or SOVRAN data, and hold each of them to this same standard in writing.
  • Keep SOVRAN work and data away from every other sub-vendor. A vendor may have other sub-vendors for its other clients; those don't need to meet this standard, but none of them may ever receive, process or see anything from SOVRAN. The vendor confirms this in writing and tells us before adding or changing any sub-vendor on our work.
  • Delete anything it held for us when the work ends, and confirm it in writing.
  • Let us check how it handles our work, and tell us within 72 hours if anything goes wrong.

2. Must not:

  • Be a data broker, people-search site, or ad-tech company, or be owned by one.
  • Place its own code, pixels or cookies on our sites or in our emails.
  • Use SOVRAN work to train AI models, build audiences, or market to the people reached.

3. The vendor register. Every vendor is listed in one place, with what it does, who approved it, the date its written agreement was signed, and the date it was last reviewed. Each vendor is reviewed at least once a year and whenever it changes owners or terms.

4. Who approves. The person who owns the area (for example, the Director of Marketing and Communications for marketing and PR vendors) chooses the vendor and is responsible for it meeting this standard. The founder can block any vendor on privacy grounds at any time.

5. Breaking the standard. A vendor that breaks this standard is stopped the same day it is found, and its access is removed. Anything it gathered must be deleted. If people were affected, we say so publicly.

Article IV: Roles and authority

Each role has real authority inside its area, and every role sits under Articles I to III. Authority to decide is never authority to track.

The Founder (maveriQ B Jackson)

  • Guards this Constitution and the Charter, and has the final word on any privacy question in any area.
  • Is the one publicly named, accountable person behind SOVRAN.
  • Signs the canary.

Director of Marketing and Communications

Authority:

  • Oversees every way SOVRAN faces the outside world: marketing, public relations, press and media, partnerships, sponsorships, events, community relations, public statements and the email list. Has the final say in all of these, including which agencies or freelancers to hire.
  • Two things stay with the founder: demands for data from any government or court (Article VI) and the canary.
  • May outsource any part of the work. Outsourcing the work never outsources the responsibility: the Lead answers for what every vendor does.

Must:

  • Hold every marketing vendor to Article III, with a signed agreement on file before work starts.
  • Approve every outside tool, link, embed and email template before it goes live, and check it loads nothing that tracks.
  • Keep the vendor register for marketing current.

Allowed (marketing that respects the Charter):

  • Earned press, interviews, podcasts and op-eds.
  • Paid placements where SOVRAN supplies a plain link and the outlet collects nothing about who clicked (for example, sponsoring a newsletter or podcast).
  • Plain-text or plain-HTML email to people who asked for it, with no open or click tracking and a one-click way out.
  • Billboards, print, radio, events and community partnerships.
  • Referral links that carry a campaign word only (like ?from=podcast), never anything about the person.

Not allowed, by anyone, in-house or outsourced:

  • Ad platforms that target, retarget or measure individual people (Meta, Google Ads conversion tracking, TikTok, X and similar), or any "pixel" or "conversion API".
  • Building or uploading audiences, lookalikes or customer lists.
  • Influencer deals that require tracking links tied to individual viewers.
  • Social media accounts in SOVRAN's name, on any platform. This is a hard rule: on those platforms, a follow, like or comment publicly ties a person to SOVRAN and hands that link to the platform, which is exactly what SOVRAN exists to prevent. The Director may propose a SOVRAN-run channel where SOVRAN controls what is gathered (nothing), through Article VII. That is a long-term idea, not a current plan.
  • A/B testing or analytics that follow individual visitors.

Every other role

Each role has its own page in the hiring packet: what it decides, what it must do, and what it may never do. The first roles are the Founder, the Director of Marketing and Communications, the Support Lead, the Lead Engineer (Witness), the Privacy and Security Officer, and the Operations and Finance Manager. Every one of them sits under Articles I to III.

Article V: Employees and contractors

  1. This Constitution is public, published next to the Charter on mysovran.online, and part of every employment and contractor agreement. Before starting, everyone reads it and the Charter and signs that they will follow both. The signed copy is kept on file.
  2. Everyone also signs the People-First Confidentiality Agreement. Its first duty is to the people SOVRAN protects: never expose, sell, trade or misuse anyone's information.
  3. Everyone gets access only to what their role needs, and loses it the day they leave.
  4. Anyone who sees a breach of this Constitution must report it to the founder. Reporting in good faith is protected: no one is punished for raising a concern, even if they turn out to be wrong.
  5. Breaking this Constitution on purpose, or hiding a breach, is grounds for removal from SOVRAN, whatever the person's role or seniority. How that is decided is set out in Article V-B.
  6. Every year, the team reviews this document together and checks every vendor against Article III.

Article V-A: Our own people's privacy

The people who work for SOVRAN get the same protection as the people who use it.

  1. Only what the law requires. SOVRAN collects from employees and contractors only what a law actually requires (for taxes, work eligibility and state reporting), plus anything a person chooses to share. Every form marks the optional parts clearly, and leaving them blank never counts against anyone.
  2. Never asked. SOVRAN never asks for photos, social media accounts, race, religion, health information, credit checks, pay history, or anything else no law requires.
  3. No copies we don't need. Identity documents are looked at in person and handed back. SOVRAN keeps no copies unless a law specifically requires one.
  4. Locked away, separately. Required records are kept encrypted, with tax and work-eligibility records kept apart from everything else, and only the Founder and the Operations and Finance Manager can open them.
  5. Deleted on schedule. Each record is destroyed the day the law stops requiring it. Every person can see what SOVRAN holds about them at any time.
  6. No monitoring. SOVRAN does not track its people: no keystroke logging, screen recording, location tracking or reading of personal accounts. Work accounts are protected for security, not used for surveillance.

Article V-B: When someone breaks these rules

Protecting people comes first, so a suspected breach is acted on the same day. A fair process comes second, so nobody is punished on a rumor.

  1. Access is frozen first. The moment a serious breach is suspected, the person's access to SOVRAN systems and information is paused while it is looked into. A pause is a safety step, not a finding of guilt.
  2. Every case goes to the Security and Leadership Review Board. The Board is the Founder and the Privacy and Security Officer, plus one more senior member of the team who has no part in the case. Anyone involved in the case steps aside, whatever their title. Until those roles are filled, the Founder reviews alone and an outside lawyer checks the decision.
  3. The person is heard. They are told in writing what they are accused of and may answer, in writing or in person, before any decision.
  4. The Board decides in writing, usually within 14 days, and records what happened, what it found, and why. A person may ask the Founder to look again once, with new information.
  5. What the Board can decide: no breach; a written warning and retraining; removal of specific access; or removal from SOVRAN.
  6. Serious breaches. When someone deliberately exposed, sold, traded or misused a person's information, or hid that someone else did: - they are removed from SOVRAN; - SOVRAN will refer the matter to law enforcement where a crime may have been committed, and may sue for damages and to stop further harm (prosecutors, not SOVRAN, decide on criminal charges); - the people affected are told what happened, as Article III requires of vendors; - they are permanently ineligible to work for SOVRAN again, or to be given access to SOVRAN work, systems or information in any form, including through a contractor, agency or vendor.
  7. How "never again" works. SOVRAN keeps a private access-exclusion record of people barred under section 6. Every vendor and contractor agreement says SOVRAN alone approves who may touch SOVRAN work, and SOVRAN declines anyone on that record. SOVRAN never publishes the record, never shares it with other employers, and never tries to stop anyone from working elsewhere: the bar covers SOVRAN only. The record holds a name, the date and the decision, nothing more, and is kept with the other required records under Article V-A, never in general systems.
  8. Never used against honest reporting. Nothing in this article may ever be used against anyone for reporting a concern in good faith, cooperating with an investigation, or anything the People-First Confidentiality Agreement protects. Doing that is itself a serious breach.

Article VI: Demands for data, and the canary

  1. Any request for user information from a government, court, police department or private party goes to the founder immediately. No one else answers it.
  2. SOVRAN gives up only what the law strictly forces it to, and challenges overly broad demands where it can. Because of Articles I and II, there should be almost nothing to give.
  3. The canary is renewed on its schedule. If SOVRAN ever receives a secret order, it is not renewed, and nobody explains why.
  4. No one may sign the canary if they are unsure every line is still true.

Article VII: Changing this Constitution

  1. This Constitution can only be changed to protect people more, never less. Any change that would collect more, track more, share more or keep data longer is not allowed, no matter who proposes it.
  2. Changes are written, approved by the founder, dated, and published with the reason.
  3. Every past version is kept, so anyone can see what changed and when.

Article VIII: Sale, merger or shutdown

  1. SOVRAN may only be sold, merged or have its control handed over if the new owner adopts this Constitution and the Charter in full, in writing, as a condition of the deal.
  2. User data is never an asset that can be sold, transferred or used as collateral.
  3. If SOVRAN shuts down, every user is told in advance and all user data is deleted. None of it goes to anyone.

Adoption

This Constitution will be written into the operating agreement of Sanctum Holdings LLC when it is formed, published at mysovran.online, and attached to every employment and contractor agreement. Until a lawyer has reviewed it for that step, it is a working draft.
