How to ask

You click a link that says you need it. That is the whole process.

There is no application. Here is a complete list of what we will never ask you for:

  • Proof of income, pay stubs, or tax returns
  • A benefits letter, EBT card, or case number
  • A referral from a caseworker, shelter, or agency
  • An explanation of your circumstances
  • A review period, an appeal, or a decision from anybody
  • Re-certification later to prove you still qualify

Here is what actually happens:

  • You choose the free option at checkout, or email us
  • We say yes
  • That is it. No follow-up, no expiry, no annual review.
Why we refuse to checkRequiring proof would mean building a database of exactly who is poor, disabled, unemployed, fleeing, or undocumented. That list is one of the most dangerous documents a person could appear on — and it is precisely the kind of thing this entire project exists to argue against.

We are not going to protect people from surveillance by surveilling them first. "We don't collect your identity" and "we don't collect your poverty" are the same commitment applied twice.

Some people who could afford it will use the free option. We know. That is a far smaller problem than one person in danger going without, and we are not going to build a machine for catching the first kind of person at the cost of the second.

How paying will work

SOVRAN+ is not open yet. When it is, here is the shape of it — written down now so you can hold us to it later.

The ruleYour payment and your account will never share an identifier. Not a customer number, not an email, not a reference code. Nothing that could be used to join the two together will exist in any database we hold.

Almost every service does the opposite: you pay, your subscription attaches to your account, and their database now links a real name and a card to everything that account ever does. That link is the honeypot all over again, and we are not building it.

Instead, you buy a token

  1. Your account already has a token. A random string like sv-4kx9-mnp2-7wqr, created the moment you sign up, with no time on it. It is not derived from your email and means nothing on its own.
  2. You buy time on a separate checkout page. No login, no account, nothing connecting the two. Our payment processor sees a card. It does not see a SOVRAN account, because none is involved.
  3. You paste the code into your account. Your account record then says one thing: paid until this date.
  4. Nothing joins them. Handed both databases, we could not tell you which customer is which user. Neither could anyone who took them from us.

Ways to pay, from ordinary to invisible

Card

Normal checkout. Card details go straight to the payment processor and never touch our servers. We would see an email and the last four digits, attached to nothing you do here.

Prepaid card

Buy a prepaid Visa or Mastercard with cash at any supermarket and use it at normal checkout. No bank account, no name, no trace back to you. Most services quietly block these. We will not.

Cash in an envelope

Write your token number on a slip of paper, put it in with cash, post it. We open it, credit the token, and that is the end of it. We never send anything back, so you never need to include a return address. The envelope holds a random number and some money and identifies nobody.

Being straight about the cash optionPost it plain and untracked, in small amounts, and understand it is at your own risk. A courier would give you tracking but requires your name and address, which destroys the only reason to do it this way. If an envelope goes missing you have lost the money and revealed nothing — that trade is the whole design.

What happens when someone cheats

Anonymous payment attracts fraud. Handling it without surveillance is possible, and it comes out cleaner than the usual approach:

  • We sell time, never lifetime access — a fraudulent payment costs a month, not forever
  • A stolen card means we void that token, not investigate a person
  • Cash and prepaid cannot be charged back at all, so the most private methods are also the safest for us
  • A token used from thirty places at once is visible as a count — we never need to know where any of them are

None of that requires knowing who you are. It never will.
