Roles at SOVRAN

Who does what, and what nobody here may ever do.

We're not hiring yet. As each role opens, a link to tell us you're interested will appear on it. Until then, this page is here so anyone can see exactly what every person at SOVRAN is responsible for, and held to.

Every role sits under the same rulesEveryone who works here signs the Constitution and the People-First Confidentiality Agreement below. No title outranks them, including the Founder's. Anyone who deliberately exposes or misuses a person's information is removed and permanently barred from SOVRAN work (Article V-B).

Role

Founder

The quick rundown

Holds the final word on every privacy question, handles every government or court demand for data personally, and signs the canary.

Required

  • Be publicly named and reachable
  • Answer every data demand personally and give up only what the law forces
  • Protect anyone who reports a problem

Never

  • Override the Charter or Constitution for any reason
  • Sell or transfer user data, or sell SOVRAN to anyone who won't adopt this Constitution
  • Punish anyone for raising a concern, or overrule the Review Board
Not hiring for this role yet

Area: SOVRAN as a whole. Guardian of the Charter and the Constitution.

What the Founder decides

  • The final word on any privacy question in any area, including blocking any vendor, tool or plan on privacy grounds.
  • Hiring, roles and the budget, until SOVRAN has a board.

Held to account by: the Security and Leadership Review Board. The Founder is not on it, can be reviewed by it, and cannot overrule it (Article V-B).

  • Changes to the Constitution, which may only ever protect people more (Article VII).

What the Founder must do

  • Be the one publicly named, findable, accountable person behind SOVRAN.
  • Handle every demand for data from a government, court, police or private party personally, give up only what the law strictly forces, and challenge broad demands (Article VI).
  • Sign the canary on schedule, and never sign it if any line is untrue.
  • Receive every report of a problem, protect whoever raised it, and act on it.
  • Review the Constitution and every vendor with the team at least once a year.

What the Founder may never do

  • Override the Charter or the Constitution, for money, growth or any other reason.
  • Sell, transfer or pledge user data, or sell SOVRAN to anyone who won't adopt the Constitution in full (Article VIII).
  • Punish anyone for reporting a concern in good faith.
Role

Director of Marketing and Communications

The quick rundown

Grows SOVRAN the honest way, through trust, press, partnerships and word of mouth, without tracking a single person. Has the final say in their area and answers for every vendor they hire.

Required

  • Hold every marketing and PR vendor to the vendor standard, in writing, before work starts
  • Check every link, page and email loads nothing that tracks
  • Report results as totals and campaign words only

Never

  • Ad platforms that target or measure individual people, or any tracking pixel
  • Building, buying or uploading audiences or customer lists
  • Social media accounts in SOVRAN's name
Not hiring for this role yet

Reports to: the Founder
Area: everything external. That covers marketing, public relations, press and media, partnerships and sponsorships, events, community relations, public statements, the website's public messaging, and the email list.

What you decide

  • SOVRAN's messaging, campaigns and public voice.
  • Which agencies, freelancers and tools to hire for your area, within the budget.
  • Press interviews, podcasts, op-eds and who speaks for SOVRAN.
  • Partnerships with organizations whose values match ours (privacy groups, legal aid, journalists, advocates).
  • What goes out on the email list, and when.

What stays with the Founder: any demand for data from a government, court or police (Constitution, Article VI), the canary, and the final word on any privacy question.

What you must do

  • Hold every marketing and PR vendor to Article III, with a signed agreement on file before any work starts, and keep your part of the vendor register current.
  • Check every link, embed, landing page and email template before it goes live, and make sure it loads nothing that tracks. When in doubt, test it with the wifi off or ask the Privacy and Security Officer.
  • Make sure every claim in our marketing is true and checkable. We never overstate what SOVRAN protects.
  • Report results the private way: total sign-ups, campaign words on links (like ?from=podcast), press mentions. Never anything about individual people.

What's allowed

  • Earned press, interviews, podcasts, op-eds and speaking.
  • Sponsoring newsletters, podcasts and events with a plain link, where the outlet collects nothing about who clicked.
  • Tracking-free email to people who asked for it, with a one-click way out.
  • Billboards, print, radio, merch, community events and partnerships.
  • Referral links that carry a campaign word only, never anything about the person.

What's never allowed (for you, your team or any vendor)

  • Ad platforms that target, retarget or measure individual people (Meta, Google Ads conversion tracking, TikTok, X and similar), or any pixel or "conversion API."
  • Building, buying or uploading audiences, lookalikes or customer lists.
  • Social media accounts in SOVRAN's name, on any platform. This is a hard rule: on those platforms, every follow, like or comment publicly ties a person to SOVRAN and hands that link to the platform. You may propose a SOVRAN-run channel where SOVRAN controls what's gathered (nothing), through the Constitution's amendment process. That's a long-term idea.
  • Influencer deals that need per-viewer tracking links.
  • A/B tests or analytics that follow individual visitors.
  • Marketing agencies or tools that profile, segment or enrich people, even if they only do it for their other clients through our campaign.

How you'll know you're doing it well

  • SOVRAN grows, and not one person was tracked to make that happen.
  • Every vendor in your area has a signed agreement on file and passes its yearly review.
  • Press and partners describe SOVRAN accurately.
Role

Support Lead

The quick rundown

Helps people, often scared ones, while learning as little about them as possible. Believes anyone who says they can't afford SOVRAN.

Required

  • Ask only what's needed to answer the question
  • Delete each conversation once it's resolved
  • Send any police or court request straight to the Founder

Never

  • Notes, tags or profiles about people who write in
  • Putting anyone's message into an AI tool
  • Asking for ID or proof of hardship
Not hiring for this role yet

Reports to: the Founder
Area: answering everyone who contacts SOVRAN, help guides, and the "nobody is turned away for lack of money" promise.

What you decide

  • How we answer: tone, saved replies, help pages and guides.
  • Which support tools we use, approved under Article III (no tools that track or profile the people writing in).
  • Who gets SOVRAN free or at a lower price. If someone says they can't afford it, we believe them. No proof, no documents, no questions (Charter, Article VIII).

What you must do

  • Ask only for what you need to answer the question in front of you, and nothing more.
  • Delete each conversation when it's resolved, unless the person asks us to keep it or the law requires it.
  • Never look anyone up, search their name, or check their social media, even to "help."
  • If someone is in danger, give them emergency contacts (911, the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, local legal aid) right away.
  • Send any request from police, a court or a government straight to the Founder. Don't answer it yourself.

What's never allowed

  • Building notes, tags, profiles or "VIP" lists about people who contact us.
  • Using AI tools or chatbots on people's messages.
  • Asking for ID, proof of hardship, or anything that confirms who someone is.
  • Sharing anything a person told us with anyone, inside or outside SOVRAN, unless it's needed to fix their problem and they agree.

How you'll know you're doing it well

  • People get real help fast, and leave knowing exactly what to do next.
  • Nothing about them is left behind afterward.
Role

Lead Engineer (Witness)

The quick rundown

Builds systems that are useless to anyone who takes them: work happens on the person's device, and everything else is sealed with keys only they hold.

Required

  • Keep only counts on the server, never what people did
  • Check every library and service for tracking before adding it
  • Document what each system stores so anyone can verify the Charter

Never

  • Logging what people search, type or upload
  • Any code that identifies, fingerprints or follows a person
  • Back doors or master keys of any kind
Not hiring for this role yet

Reports to: the Founder
Area: the Witness first, then the removal engine and SOVRAN's apps and sites.

What you decide

  • How SOVRAN's products are built: architecture, languages, hosting and libraries, within the Constitution.
  • Engineering tools and services, approved under Article III.
  • When something is ready to ship, together with the Privacy and Security Officer.

What you must do

  • Build so that SOVRAN can't read what it doesn't need to: work on the person's device whenever possible, and encrypt everything else with keys only the person holds.
  • Keep only counts on the server, never what people searched or did (Charter, Article IV).
  • Check every library, SDK and service for tracking before adding it. No analytics SDKs, ad SDKs, crash reporters that collect device IDs, or "free" services that pay for themselves with data.
  • Make sure the Witness keeps footage safe and out of reach, including from SOVRAN, unless the person chooses to share it.
  • Write down what each system stores, so anyone can check the Charter is true.

What's never allowed

  • Logging what people search, type, watch or upload.
  • Any code that identifies, fingerprints or follows a person or device.
  • A "temporary" debugging switch that collects real user data.
  • Back doors, master keys, or any way for SOVRAN to read a person's sealed data. A lost passphrase can't be recovered by anyone, and that's on purpose.

How you'll know you're doing it well

  • Every Charter promise is true because of how the system is built, not because of a policy.
  • If SOVRAN's servers were seized tomorrow, there would be nothing useful on them.
Role

Privacy and Security Officer

The quick rundown

Checks the Charter is true in practice, enforces the vendor standard, and keeps SOVRAN secure. Can pause anything that might break the Constitution until the Founder decides.

Required

  • Keep the vendor register and review every vendor yearly
  • Test the sites and emails for tracking regularly
  • Lead the Security and Leadership Review Board, which can review anyone, including the Founder

Never

  • Approving an exception to the Constitution: there are none
  • Security logs that record what individual people did
  • Quietly fixing a breach without telling anyone
Not hiring for this role yet

Reports to: the Founder (this can start as a part-time or contract role)
Area: keeping the Charter true, the vendor standard enforced, and SOVRAN secure.

What you decide

  • Whether a vendor, tool or feature meets the Constitution. You can pause anything that might not, until the Founder decides.
  • Security practices: access, passwords, two-step sign-in, device rules.
  • What happens when something goes wrong: the incident plan.

What you must do

  • Keep the vendor register, run each vendor's yearly review, and check that every vendor touching SOVRAN work has a signed Article III agreement.
  • Test our sites and emails regularly to confirm they load nothing that tracks.
  • Run security checks on what Engineering builds before it ships.
  • Lead the response to any breach or mistake: stop it, fix it, tell the people affected, and say so publicly.
  • Lead the Security and Leadership Review Board (Article V-B). The Board reviews everyone, including the Founder, and on Board matters you answer to the Constitution, not to the Founder.
  • Run the yearly Constitution review with the team.

What's never allowed

  • Approving an exception to the Constitution. There are no exceptions.
  • Keeping a security log that records what individual users did. Logs record that something happened, never who or what they searched.
  • Quietly fixing a breach without telling the people affected.

How you'll know you're doing it well

  • Every vendor is on the register, signed and reviewed.
  • Every Charter promise could pass an outside audit tomorrow.

Operations and Finance Manager

The quick rundown

Runs SOVRAN properly and legally, and makes sure money tools never become a backdoor for tracking the people we serve.

Required

  • Keep accurate books and file taxes on time
  • Use payment tools that learn the legal minimum about anyone
  • Delete backer details once rewards are delivered

Never

  • Taking money from anyone whose business depends on data or advertising
  • Selling, sharing or pledging customer or backer lists
  • Asking anyone for proof of income
Not hiring for this role yet

Reports to: the Founder
Area: budget, books, taxes, payments, Kickstarter fulfillment, contracts and the vendor paperwork.

What you decide

  • Day-to-day spending within the budget.
  • Accounting, banking, payroll and payment tools, approved under Article III.
  • How Kickstarter rewards are delivered.

What you must do

  • Keep accurate books and file taxes on time for Sanctum Holdings LLC.
  • Choose payment processing that keeps what SOVRAN learns about a paying person to the legal minimum, and never connects payments to what people do in SOVRAN.
  • Make sure every vendor contract includes the Article III terms, and keep signed copies with the vendor register.
  • Collect only the backer details needed to deliver a reward, and delete them once it's delivered.
  • Keep signed Constitution and Confidentiality acknowledgments on file for everyone.

What's never allowed

  • Taking money from anyone whose business depends on data or advertising (Charter, Article V).
  • Selling, sharing or pledging customer or backer lists, including as loan collateral (Constitution, Article VIII).
  • Asking anyone for proof of income to get help. If they say they can't pay, they don't pay (Charter, Article VIII).

How you'll know you're doing it well

  • SOVRAN is financially healthy, legally clean and audit-ready.
  • Our money trail says nothing about who our users are.

Your privacy when you work here

SOVRAN protects the people who work for it the same way it protects the people who use it. We only ask for what a law actually requires to hire and pay you, plus anything you choose to share. Every form marks the optional parts clearly, and leaving them blank never counts against you.

  • No copies of your ID. You show documents in person, we write down what the law asks, and hand them back.
  • Kept locked and apart. Required records are encrypted, kept separately, and only two people can open them.
  • Deleted on time. Each record is destroyed the day the law stops requiring it, and you can see what we hold about you at any time.
  • No monitoring. No keystroke logging, screen recording, location tracking, or reading your personal accounts.

Example: the form every new hire fills out

SOVRAN asks for as little about you as the law allows. Everything on this form is in one of three groups. Only the first one is required.

REQUIRED BY LAW

We can't legally hire or pay you without these. Each one says which law needs it.

  • Legal name. Needed for your W-4, Form I-9 and Colorado's new-hire report.
  • Home address. Needed for your W-4 and Colorado's new-hire report.
  • Social Security number. Needed for your W-4 (federal taxes) and Colorado's new-hire report.
  • Date of birth. Needed for Colorado's new-hire report and Form I-9.
  • Form W-4. Federal tax withholding. You choose your own filing status.
  • Form I-9. Proof you can work in the U.S. You show original documents in person. We look, write down what the law asks, and hand them back. We do not keep copies.

Contractors instead fill out a Form W-9. Tip: you can get a free EIN from the IRS and use it instead of your Social Security number.

OPTIONAL: YOU NEVER HAVE TO FILL THIS IN

Leave any of these blank. Nothing changes for you, ever.

  • The name you'd like us to use, if it's different from your legal name:
  • Emergency contact (someone we call if you're hurt at work):
  • Direct deposit. Colorado law says it can't be required. Without it, you're paid by check. Bank and account:
  • Phone number (we can always reach you by your work email instead):

NEVER ASKED

SOVRAN will never ask for: a photo of you, your social media, your race, religion or health information, a credit check, a background check, your marital status (beyond the W-4 filing status you choose), or your pay at past jobs. Colorado law bars employers from asking about pay history.

People-First Confidentiality Agreement

Most confidentiality agreements protect a company's secrets. This one protects people first.

SOVRAN exists so that people can't be tracked, profiled or sold. The worst thing anyone working here could do isn't leaking a business plan. It's exposing, selling or misusing the information of a person who trusted us. So that is the first and biggest promise in this agreement.

Between: Sanctum Holdings LLC, doing business as SOVRAN ("SOVRAN"), and the person signing below ("you"). This applies whether you're an employee, contractor, freelancer, intern or volunteer.

1. Your first duty: protect people

You will never, during your work with SOVRAN or at any time after:

  • share, sell, trade, give away or publish any information about a SOVRAN user, a visitor to our sites, or anyone who contacts us;
  • look up, research, follow or try to identify any of those people, for any reason;
  • copy any of that information to a personal device, account, email, notes app, AI tool or anywhere outside SOVRAN's approved systems;
  • use anything you learned about a person through SOVRAN for your own benefit, or for anyone else's;
  • help, ignore or cover for anyone, inside or outside SOVRAN, who does any of the above.

This duty never ends. It continues after you leave SOVRAN, for as long as you live.

If you're ever unsure whether something would break this promise, stop and ask the founder first.

2. Your second duty: protect SOVRAN's private business information

You will keep private anything SOVRAN hasn't made public: unreleased plans and products, security details (keys, passwords, system designs, weaknesses), vendor terms, finances, and internal discussions. Use it only for your SOVRAN work, and return or delete it when you leave.

This doesn't cover anything that is already public, that you knew before SOVRAN, or that you learned elsewhere without breaking a promise to anyone.

3. What this agreement never stops you from doing

Nothing here stops you from:

  • reporting a crime, a safety risk, or a possible violation of the law to any government agency, regulator, court or lawyer, or taking part in their investigation;
  • telling the founder, a regulator or the public that SOVRAN itself broke its Charter or Constitution;
  • talking about your own pay, hours or working conditions, or acting together with coworkers about them, as the law protects.

Under the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act, you can't be held liable for sharing a trade secret in confidence with a government official or a lawyer only to report or investigate a suspected violation of law, or in a sealed court filing.

4. If you break it

A suspected breach is reviewed by SOVRAN's Security and Leadership Review Board, as set out in Article V-B of the Constitution. Your access may be paused while that happens. You'll be told in writing what the concern is and get to answer before anything is decided.

If the Board finds you deliberately broke section 1:

  • you'll be removed from SOVRAN;
  • SOVRAN will refer the matter to law enforcement where a crime may have been committed, and may sue for damages and to stop further harm;
  • the people affected will be told;
  • you'll be permanently barred from working for SOVRAN or touching SOVRAN work again, including through any contractor, agency or vendor.

That bar covers SOVRAN only. SOVRAN will never publish it or try to stop you from working anywhere else.

Breaking section 2 can also lead to removal and legal action.

5. The rest

This agreement works together with the SOVRAN Constitution, which you also sign. If anything here seems to conflict with the Constitution, whichever protects people more wins. If a court finds one part of this agreement unenforceable, the rest still stands. Colorado law governs this agreement.
